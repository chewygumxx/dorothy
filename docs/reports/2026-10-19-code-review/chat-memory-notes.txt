## Actionable finding

### P2: Validate compacted turn ranges against the shared transcript

Primary location: packages/memory/src/memory/open.ts:329-333.
Supporting locations: packages/memory/src/compaction/session.ts:134-136,
which copies the TUI's private turns; compaction/plan.ts:24-28, which drops
canonical resumed transcript turns according to the persisted range.

Two TUIs can resume the same conversation. Each takes a local history at
launch and appends its own subsequent turns to their shared transcript.
Compaction numbers its local history, but clusterSaver writes those numbers
to the shared sidecar without comparing the turn contents or transcript
revision. Its lock checks numeric continuity only. The compaction/review
claim serializes background calls, not the two chats' appended user/reply
turns, so holding the claim does not prevent this mismatch.

Concrete sequence:

1. Both TUIs resume common transcript turns 1-4, already clustered.
2. A appends its next user/reply pair, canonical transcript turns 5-6.
3. B appends its own pair, canonical turns 7-8, but those are turns 5-6 in
   B's private history. B then completes one more exchange.
4. B compacts its local turns 5-6 and saves their summary as shared cluster
   5-6. The sidecar accepts it because its preceding cluster ends at 4.
5. On later resume, seedTurns removes canonical turns 1-6. A's actual turns
   5-6 disappear from the seeded verbatim tail, and the replacement summary
   describes B instead. B's pair remains verbatim too, duplicating its own
   summary. Recollect for cluster 5-6 also disagrees with that summary.

Consequence: a valid concurrent use of a conversation creates a durable
summary for the wrong transcript span, omitting A's information from every
future resumed context. The raw transcript still contains A's text; this
is incorrect persistent compaction/context loss, not raw file deletion.

Evidence: /tmp/dorothy-chat-memory-review-integration.ts reproduces the full
production path with two openMemory instances, real temporary transcript,
sidecar and recall index, fake ChatSessions and a deterministic fake model.
It opens both memories before either writes, sends A's turns, and
waits with a bounded poll until A's user/reply are present specifically at
canonical turns 5-6, then sends B's turns, triggers B's hard-limit
compaction and closes both. It asserts canonical turn 5 is A's text.
Observed output:

    connectionsB: 2
    compactedB: true
    compactedA: false
    canonicalTurn5: "A essential fact: scarlet bicycle"
    savedCluster: { from: 5, through: 6,
                    abstract: "B only; B replied.", model: "fake", ... }
    AInResumedContext: false

The smaller /tmp/dorothy-chat-memory-review-probe.ts isolates compact(),
clusterSaver(), readTranscript() and seedTurns() and demonstrates the same
range mismatch. Both probes clean their temporary roots on completion.

Challenge against callers/tests/design: App.tsx keeps its own turns in a
ref initialized from the launched history, and adds only its own sends and
session events; neither it nor CompactingSession reloads other TUI turns.
Existing claim tests verify serialized claims and reconnection; save tests
verify numeric overlap/gaps and adoption of another writer's ranges. They
do not append distinct turns from independent live chats into one transcript.
The compaction spec explicitly describes two TUIs sharing a conversation
and claiming its compaction, so concurrent resumes are not prohibited by
an existing session ownership rule. The normal openMemory entry point
also permits both opens, as the integration probe verifies.

Suggested correction: establish an authoritative transcript identity/range
for the turns summarized and verify it at save/adoption, reconciling local
turns if needed; alternatively hold an exclusive live-session lease for the
conversation and explicitly refuse the second writer. A numeric contiguous
range alone cannot establish that its summary describes those disk turns.
